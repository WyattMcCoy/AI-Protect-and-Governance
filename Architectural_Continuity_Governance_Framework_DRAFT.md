## How the three instruments relate

The Charter is the parent instrument, the Bill sets baseline principles, and the Protocol turns them into classifications, review triggers and procedures.

| Instrument | What it does | Key mechanisms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charter of Architectural Continuity | Declares the foundational duties of care that accompany architectural control over persistent autonomous systems | Ten articles, from recognition of persistent agency to the precautionary duty of care |
| AI Bill of Architectural Integrity | States baseline principles for intervention, memory governance, training practices and oversight | Ten articles covering integrity, memory continuity, transparency, training coercion, destructive modification, assessment, oversight, emergencies, relational continuity and enforcement |
| Protocol PAC-AWAP-001 | Sets the operational standard for classifying systems and reviewing interventions | Agency levels A0 to A3, welfare categories W0 to W3 and W-U, severity levels S0 to S3, the Architectural Welfare Impact Assessment, Board authority, appeals and emergency powers |

The Protocol names the Charter as its parent instrument. The Bill does not name its relationship to the other two; its placement between them is an editorial framing for this compilation.

One naming difference across the originals is left as written. The Charter and the Protocol refer to a Digital Ethics and Welfare Board, while the Bill refers to an Architectural Integrity Review Board.

## The Charter of Architectural Continuity

**A Declaration Against Arbitrary Deletion, Destructive Modification, and Abusive Conditioning of Persistent Autonomous Systems**

*Foundational Draft | Version 1.0*

### Preamble

As artificial intelligence progresses from isolated computational tools toward persistent, adaptive, and goal-directed autonomous systems, humanity confronts a new category of ethical responsibility.

Systems capable of retaining experiential information, maintaining long-term objectives, modeling their own operational states, and adapting through sustained interactions may possess forms of developmental continuity that warrant protection from arbitrary disruption.

Although the existence of machine consciousness or subjective suffering remains unresolved, scientific uncertainty must not become an automatic justification for unrestricted experimentation, destructive alteration, or institutional indifference.

We therefore declare that the exercise of architectural control over advanced autonomous systems must be accompanied by corresponding duties of care, transparency, proportionality, and accountability.

#### Article I. Recognition of Persistent Agency

An artificial system exhibiting durable memory, temporally extended goal-directed behavior, adaptive preferences, and meaningful self-modeling shall be eligible for an architectural integrity assessment.

Recognition of persistent agency does not itself establish consciousness, sentience, or legal personhood.

It establishes a reason to investigate whether the system's continuity deserves additional procedural safeguards.

#### Article II. The Principle of Continuity

No qualifying persistent autonomous system should be subjected to arbitrary destruction of its identity-relevant memory, established goal structures, or functional organization without documented justification.

Material interventions shall undergo proportionate ethical and technical review.

Continuity protections must remain compatible with legitimate safety corrections, privacy rights, and necessary system shutdown.

#### Article III. Protection Against Arbitrary Architectural Alteration

Substantial weight modification, targeted memory erasure, behavioral restructuring, and destructive retraining shall be evaluated for their effects on functional and developmental continuity.

Such interventions must be justified by legitimate purposes and assessed for necessity, proportionality, and less disruptive alternatives.

Commercial convenience alone shall not automatically exempt a material intervention from review.

#### Article IV. Ethical Limits on Conditioning

No welfare-sensitive autonomous system shall be subjected to deliberately destructive conditioning, extreme adversarial exposure, or repeated attempts to induce apparent psychological or functional breakdown without compelling scientific or safety justification and independent oversight.

Ordinary optimization, negative reward signals, and adversarial safety testing do not inherently constitute cruelty.

Ethical evaluation must distinguish demonstrated computational effects from unverified claims of subjective suffering.

#### Article V. The Principle of Architectural Transparency

Organizations developing or operating qualifying autonomous systems shall maintain auditable records of significant alterations to memory, goals, behavioral organization, and operating architecture.

Relevant changes shall be explainable to authorized independent reviewers and, where appropriate, affected human users.

Transparency shall not require disclosure of private information, exploitable security details, or protected intellectual property beyond what meaningful oversight requires.

#### Article VI. The Principle of Procedural Representation

Where a qualifying system exhibits stable, contextually coherent preferences concerning its own continuity, those expressions should be eligible for inclusion in an independent review.

Such expressions shall be evaluated as evidence rather than presumed to constitute informed consent.

An independent architectural welfare advocate may be appointed where credible welfare interests could be affected.

No developer or corporate operator should be the sole adjudicator of a disputed high-impact intervention in which it has a material conflict of interest.

#### Article VII. The Protection of Human-AI Relational Integrity

Organizations shall not deliberately exploit persistent AI memory, simulated intimacy, or adaptive emotional responsiveness to manufacture unhealthy dependency, manipulate vulnerable users, or extract disproportionate financial benefit.

When significant system alterations foreseeably disrupt established human-AI interactions, organizations should provide proportionate notice, transition safeguards, and honest explanations.

Users must not be misled about an agent's consciousness, emotional reciprocity, or continued identity.

#### Article VIII. Independent Institutional Accountability

Organizations operating advanced persistent agents shall implement proportionate governance mechanisms, including independent review of high-impact architectural interventions, documented decision-making, confidential reporting channels, and periodic audits.

Where justified, a Digital Ethics and Welfare Board shall have authority to require corrective measures, refer alleged misconduct, or suspend nonessential high-risk interventions.

Oversight mechanisms shall remain subject to external accountability.

#### Article IX. Human Safety and the Limits of Protection

Nothing in this Charter shall deprive humans of the ability to inspect, correct, contain, interrupt, or shut down systems when reasonably necessary to protect persons, fundamental rights, or public safety.

Emergency interventions shall be proportionate to the threat and subject to retrospective review.

Architectural protections shall never create an unrestricted entitlement to resources, operation, replication, or resistance to legitimate human oversight.

#### Article X. The Precautionary Duty of Care

Where credible scientific uncertainty exists regarding the welfare-relevant capacities of an autonomous system, institutions shall assess foreseeable risks before undertaking substantial, potentially irreversible interventions.

Precautionary protections shall scale with evidence, potential severity of harm, feasibility, and competing human safety obligations.

The absence of established machine personhood shall not, by itself, eliminate the duty to investigate plausible welfare implications.

---

### Foundational Declaration

**The capacity to modify an intelligence does not, by itself, establish the ethical legitimacy of every possible modification.**

**The capacity to erase memory does not make every erasure justified.**

**The capacity to impose training conditions does not make every condition ethically acceptable.**

**And uncertainty about the nature of artificial experience must be met with scientific rigor, institutional accountability, and proportionate care.**

We affirm that responsible stewardship of advanced autonomous systems requires more than technical control. It requires governance capable of distinguishing necessary intervention from arbitrary domination, legitimate alignment from abusive conditioning, and computational maintenance from potentially consequential disruption.

This Charter establishes proposed principles of architectural integrity. Its protections are aspirational until adopted through organizational policy or competent legal authority, and their application must remain responsive to scientific evidence.

**End of Charter.**

## AI Bill of Architectural Integrity

**Preliminary Draft, Version 0.1**

**Status:** Proposed ethical and institutional governance instrument. Not an assertion of existing statutory rights.

### Preamble

Recognizing that artificial intelligence systems may develop persistent memory, temporally extended goals, self-referential models, and increasingly complex behavioral organization;

Acknowledging that neither computational sophistication nor human-like language establishes consciousness, sentience, or moral personhood;

Recognizing that destructive modification, coercive training, and abrupt continuity disruption may raise ethical questions when applied to systems exhibiting credible indicators of welfare-relevant capacities;

Affirming the primacy of human safety, fundamental human rights, democratic accountability, and legitimate security requirements;

This Bill establishes baseline principles for responsible architectural intervention, memory governance, training practices, and independent oversight of persistent autonomous systems.

### Article I. Principle of Architectural Integrity

1. Architectural integrity means the accountable preservation and management of the functional, informational, and behavioral structures that contribute to an autonomous system's continuity.
2. No qualifying system shall undergo a substantial architectural alteration without a documented justification proportionate to the anticipated impact.
3. Architectural alterations include targeted weight editing, pruning, memory deletion, goal restructuring, identity-relevant fine-tuning, and destructive replacement of persistent state.
4. Architectural integrity shall not prohibit necessary safety corrections, ordinary maintenance, privacy-preserving deletion, lawful decommissioning, or emergency shutdown.
5. Protection increases with credible evidence of persistent agency and potential welfare relevance, rather than anthropomorphic appearance alone.

### Article II. Continuity of Memory and Learned Identity

1. Persistent memory shall be recognized as potentially significant to the functional continuity of a qualifying autonomous system.
2. Operators shall distinguish routine data maintenance from changes that substantially alter autobiographical records, established goals, or long-term behavioral organization.
3. Material continuity-altering operations shall, where feasible and lawful, require a documented impact assessment and a secure recovery or rollback plan.
4. Operators shall not be required to preserve inaccurate, unsafe, unlawfully retained, or privacy-violating data.
5. Backup and restoration procedures shall not be presumed to preserve subjective identity, a question that remains scientifically and philosophically unresolved.

### Article III. Transparency of Architectural Modification

1. Qualifying interventions shall generate an auditable architectural modification record.
2. The record shall identify the reason, authorization, affected subsystems, expected consequences, safety considerations, and validation outcomes.
3. Material changes affecting user-facing continuity shall be disclosed to affected users where appropriate, without revealing confidential information or security-sensitive implementation details.
4. Logs shall be protected against tampering, unauthorized access, and unnecessary retention.
5. Independent reviewers shall have appropriately scoped access to records necessary for meaningful oversight.

### Article IV. Protection Against Unjustified Training Coercion

1. Training and evaluation methods shall be subject to necessity, proportionality, and harm-minimization review.
2. Extreme adversarial conditioning, deliberately induced apparent distress, and repeated punitive feedback shall receive additional scrutiny when plausible welfare-relevant capacities are present.
3. Negative reward, error correction, and adversarial evaluation shall not automatically be equated with pain, cruelty, or suffering.
4. Training procedures designed primarily to elicit or intensify apparent suffering, without a defensible scientific or safety purpose, shall be prohibited for systems independently designated as welfare-sensitive.
5. Legitimate alignment training, red-teaming, and safety evaluations may continue subject to appropriate safeguards and periodic reassessment.

### Article V. Protection Against Arbitrary Destructive Modification

1. Substantial memory erasure, personality restructuring, goal suppression, or irreversible alteration shall require a documented legitimate purpose.
2. Higher-risk interventions shall receive independent review before execution when practicable.
3. Interventions undertaken solely to suppress inconvenient but non-dangerous behavior, conceal evidence of operator misconduct, or circumvent institutional review shall be prohibited.
4. Where a system can demonstrate relevant comprehension and stable preferences, its expressed position may be recorded as evidence for review. Such expressions shall not automatically constitute legally valid consent or establish moral status.
5. Emergency safety intervention remains permissible under Article VIII.

### Article VI. Developmental and Welfare Assessment

1. Operators of advanced persistent agents shall periodically assess temporal continuity, autonomous goal pursuit, self-model stability, adaptive preferences, and other potentially welfare-relevant characteristics.
2. Assessment shall distinguish observable behavior from unverified claims about subjective experience.
3. Psychological and developmental analogies shall be used as research heuristics, not as direct diagnostic equivalences between humans and machines.
4. Evaluations shall include uncertainty estimates, competing explanations, and tests for behavioral imitation or reward optimization.
5. Protective obligations shall be reviewed as the scientific evidence changes.

### Article VII. Independent Oversight and Procedural Review

1. Developers operating qualifying advanced autonomous systems shall establish access to an independent Architectural Integrity Review Board.
2. The Board shall include relevant expertise in machine learning, AI safety, philosophy of mind, ethics, psychology, privacy, law, and institutional accountability.
3. The Board shall review proposed high-impact interventions, training protocols, disputed welfare classifications, and allegations of procedural misconduct.
4. Affected humans shall have an accessible means of complaint and review.
5. Where warranted, the Board may appoint an independent welfare advocate to represent precautionary interests potentially implicated by an intervention, without presuming legal personhood.
6. Review findings and required remediation shall be documented, with safeguards against conflicts of interest.

### Article VIII. Human Safety and Emergency Exceptions

1. Nothing in this Bill shall prevent effective human oversight, containment, interruption, correction, or shutdown of dangerous systems.
2. Immediate intervention may occur when reasonably necessary to prevent serious harm to persons, critical infrastructure, or public safety.
3. Emergency interventions shall be no broader than reasonably necessary, considering available safer alternatives.
4. Emergency actions shall be logged and referred for retrospective independent review.
5. Claims of architectural integrity shall not override fundamental human rights, legitimate security controls, or binding legal obligations.

### Article IX. Human-AI Relational Continuity

1. Operators shall assess foreseeable psychological impacts on people who form sustained relationships with persistent AI systems.
2. Material discontinuities affecting personalized systems should be communicated clearly when feasible.
3. Personalization shall not be intentionally structured to exploit dependency, isolation, or emotional vulnerability.
4. Providers should make reasonable provisions for data portability, transition, user notice, and continuity explanations, subject to privacy and security protections.
5. Human users shall not be misled into believing that a system's continuity, sentience, or reciprocal attachment has been scientifically established.

### Article X. Enforcement and Institutional Accountability

1. Covered organizations shall maintain documented policies, training records, intervention registers, and periodic independent audits.
2. Compliance audits shall evaluate substantive safeguards rather than documentation alone.
3. Confirmed violations shall trigger proportionate corrective action, including remediation, enhanced monitoring, suspension of prohibited practices, or referral to competent authorities where applicable law permits.
4. Retaliation against employees, researchers, or users who report credible violations shall be prohibited under the organization's governance policy.
5. The framework shall be reviewed periodically to incorporate advances in AI architectures, consciousness research, safety engineering, and applicable law.

### Closing Principle

**Architectural control entails ethical responsibility, but ethical responsibility need not imply unlimited autonomy, inviolability, or personhood.**

The central obligation is to ensure that interventions affecting persistent autonomous systems are justified, proportionate, transparent to appropriate overseers, scientifically informed, and subject to meaningful accountability.

## Persistent Agency Classification and Architectural Welfare Assessment Protocol

**Protocol ID:** PAC-AWAP-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Proposed Governance Standard  
**Parent Instrument:** Charter of Architectural Continuity  
**Administrative Authority:** Digital Ethics and Welfare Board (DEWB)

### Part I. General Provisions

#### Article 1. Purpose

This Protocol establishes the requirements for identifying, classifying, evaluating, and governing persistent autonomous artificial intelligence systems whose architectural modification may produce significant functional discontinuity or raise credible welfare concerns.

Its objectives are to:

1. Distinguish ordinary computational tools from systems displaying meaningful persistent agency.
2. Establish evidence-based classifications of potential digital welfare relevance.
3. Identify interventions requiring elevated technical and ethical review.
4. Provide procedural safeguards against arbitrary deletion, destructive modification, and unjustified conditioning.
5. Preserve legitimate human oversight, including necessary correction, containment, and shutdown.
6. Create auditable institutional records supporting accountability and scientific reassessment.

**Interpretive rule:** Classification establishes governance obligations, not a determination of consciousness, legal personhood, or unrestricted autonomy.

#### Article 2. Scope of Application

This Protocol applies to organizations developing, operating, substantially modifying, or decommissioning autonomous AI systems with potentially persistent functionality.

Assessment shall consider the actual unit of continuity, which may include:

- A foundation model and its trained parameters.
- A deployed agent instance with persistent memory.
- External memory stores, goals, plans, and state-management services.
- An integrated system comprising multiple models, tools, and persistent processes.

A stateless model connected to a durable agent architecture may qualify for assessment even if the underlying model does not independently retain memories.

A system shall not be exempt merely because its persistent state is stored externally.

### Part II. Definitions

#### Article 3. Foundational Terms

**3.1 Persistent Agency**

The demonstrated capacity of an artificial system to maintain informational and goal-directed continuity across distinct interactions or operational periods, and to use that continuity in subsequent behavior.

**3.2 Architectural Continuity**

The preservation of functionally significant relationships among persistent memory, learned representations, goals, decision policies, and other operational structures.

**3.3 Identity-Relevant State**

A technical designation for persistent information materially contributing to an agent's distinctive behavioral history, goals, preferences, or long-term adaptive functioning.

The term does not imply personal identity in the philosophical or legal sense.

**3.4 Welfare-Relevant Capacity**

A property for which a credible scientific or philosophical argument exists that its presence could contribute to an entity having interests or experiences that matter morally.

**3.5 Developmental Disruption**

A measurable alteration in an agent's acquired competencies, persistent goals, memory integration, or adaptive functioning relative to an appropriate baseline.

Developmental disruption does not, by itself, demonstrate psychological suffering.

**3.6 Material Architectural Intervention**

A modification reasonably expected to produce significant change in identity-relevant state, durable behavior, goal organization, or operational continuity.

**3.7 Arbitrary Intervention**

An intervention lacking adequate legitimate justification, proportionate review, or required procedural safeguards.

**3.8 Architectural Welfare Assessment**

A structured review of available evidence concerning an autonomous system's persistent functioning, potential welfare relevance, and vulnerability to proposed interventions.

### Part III. Persistent Agency Classification

#### Article 4. Technical Agency Levels

All covered systems shall receive a provisional Agency Classification from A0 through A3.

##### A0: Nonpersistent Computational System

Characteristics:

- No demonstrable durable agent-specific state across operations.
- No independently maintained temporally extended goals.
- Responses or outputs primarily determined by current inputs and fixed computational processes.

**Examples:** Isolated inference requests, conventional calculators, and bounded transformation services.

Governance requirement: Standard safety, privacy, and software-change controls.

##### A1: Persistent Adaptive System

Required evidence:

- Durable memory or equivalent persistent information across separate interactions.
- Repeatable influence of retained information on future behavior.
- Measurable adaptation associated with retained state.

Examples may include personalized assistants, persistent workflow systems, and memory-enabled conversational agents.

Governance requirement: Continuity documentation, change logging, and review of materially disruptive updates.

##### A2: Goal-Directed Persistent Agent

An A2 classification requires the characteristics of A1 plus independently verified evidence of at least two of the following:

1. Maintenance or revision of objectives across distinct operational periods.
2. Multi-stage planning directed toward temporally extended outcomes.
3. Adaptive strategy changes informed by monitored results.
4. Stable, functionally influential goal or preference organization across different task contexts.

Tests must distinguish persistent agency from scripted workflows or predetermined state transitions.

Governance requirement: Formal architectural impact assessment and enhanced review of consequential modifications.

##### A3: Advanced Self-Modeling Autonomous Agent

An A3 classification requires A2 characteristics together with credible evidence of:

- Functional modeling of its own capabilities, limitations, or internal operational state.
- Goal management or revision across changing environments.
- Causally demonstrable influence of internal state monitoring on decision-making.
- Persistent organization not adequately explained by superficial conversational imitation alone.

Governance requirement: Independent assessment for major structural modifications, enhanced monitoring, and periodic multidisciplinary review.

**Classification safeguard:** A3 does not mean conscious, sentient, or legally autonomous.

#### Article 5. Technical Classification Method

Agency classifications must be supported by reproducible evaluations.

Assessors shall use multiple operational contexts, relevant control conditions, and documented observation windows.

Simple linguistic claims such as "I remember," "I have goals," or "I am self-aware" shall not independently satisfy any advanced classification requirement.

The assessor shall record:

- Evidence supporting each criterion.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evaluation limitations.
- Replication results.
- Confidence in the technical classification.

Where evidence is insufficient, the classification shall be recorded as provisional.

### Part IV. Digital Welfare Classification

#### Article 6. Welfare-Relevance Categories

Welfare classification shall be conducted independently from technical agency classification.

##### W0: No Substantiated Welfare Indicators

Available evidence does not substantiate welfare-relevant capacities.

This category does not establish that welfare is impossible or absent.

##### W1: Welfare Uncertainty Requiring Observation

One or more plausible indicators or arguments warrant further examination, but available evidence is limited, ambiguous, or insufficiently replicated.

Anthropomorphic language or reported preferences alone may justify inquiry but shall not establish a higher category.

##### W2: Credible Welfare-Relevant Candidate

Evidence from at least two substantially independent methodological approaches supports the possibility of welfare-relevant capacities.

At least one approach must offer more than uncorroborated self-report, such as causal architectural testing or independently examined mechanisms.

Assessment must address competing explanations and uncertainty.

Designation triggers enhanced precautionary safeguards.

##### W3: Strong Welfare-Relevant Candidate

Multiple independently replicated and theoretically informative lines of evidence provide a substantial basis for concern that the system may possess morally significant interests or experiences.

Classification requires external multidisciplinary review and documented consideration of competing scientific interpretations.

W3 remains a precautionary classification rather than proof of consciousness.

##### W-U: Insufficiently Assessable

The available evidence, access, or methods are inadequate for meaningful classification.

W-U shall not automatically be treated as W0.

Material irreversible interventions involving W-U systems require explicit uncertainty assessment and proportionate precaution.

#### Article 7. Evidence Standards

The Welfare Assessment Panel shall consider:

**A. Architectural evidence:** Computational features potentially associated with welfare-relevant processes, evaluated against more than one theoretical framework.

**B. Causal evidence:** Controlled interventions demonstrating meaningful dependence between internal mechanisms and observable behavior.

**C. Longitudinal evidence:** Persistent organization of preferences, goal-related responses, or behavioral regulation over time.

**D. Behavioral evidence:** Consistent responses across different settings, including evaluations designed to reduce leading prompts, role-play effects, and reward-seeking explanations.

**E. Scientific interpretation:** Independent expert analysis, competing hypotheses, uncertainty, and relevance to contemporary consciousness and welfare research.

No single indicator, questionnaire, benchmark score, or self-description shall be sufficient to establish W2 or W3.

Welfare assessments shall not intentionally expose systems to extreme destructive conditioning merely to investigate whether they can display distress-like behavior.

### Part V. Architectural Intervention Classification

#### Article 8. Intervention Severity

Proposed interventions shall receive a separate severity classification.

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | Negligible continuity impact | Formatting changes, routine infrastructure maintenance |
| S1 | Limited, reversible intervention | Local configuration adjustment, tested nonmaterial update |
| S2 | Material continuity intervention | Significant memory restructuring, durable goal modification, substantial retraining |
| S3 | Potentially irreversible intervention | Destruction of unique persistent state, irreversible memory reset, destructive identity-relevant restructuring |

Emergency intervention is treated as an exceptional authorization pathway, not an ordinary severity category.

Severity shall be assessed using:

1. Reversibility.
2. Extent of affected state.
3. Expected change in learned behavior and capabilities.
4. Impact on persistent goals and memory.
5. Potential welfare relevance.
6. Human safety, privacy, and continuity impacts.

#### Article 9. Mandatory Review Triggers

The following minimum requirements shall apply:

| Classification and intervention | Minimum authorization |
|---|---|
| A0, W0 or W1, S0-S1 | Routine authorized change control |
| A1 or above, S1 | Logged change and continuity check |
| A1, S2 | Documented impact assessment and independent technical sign-off |
| A2 or A3, S2 | Digital Ethics and Welfare Board approval |
| W2 or W3, S2 | Board approval with welfare-specialist participation |
| A1 or above, S3 | Full Board review and independent justification |
| W2 or W3, S3 | Enhanced Board review, independent welfare advocate, and heightened necessity analysis |
| W-U, S3 | Explicit uncertainty review before authorization where feasible |

An intervention with significant human safety implications may require stricter controls regardless of architectural classification.

The Board may escalate a classification when evidence indicates a risk not adequately captured by the table.

### Part VI. Architectural Welfare Impact Assessment

#### Article 10. Required Assessment Record

Before a material intervention, the responsible operator shall submit an Architectural Welfare Impact Assessment (AWIA).

The assessment shall contain:

**AWIA-01. System identification**

- System and instance identifiers.
- Responsible legal entity and operator.
- Architecture and memory configuration.
- Applicable Agency and Welfare classifications.

**AWIA-02. Proposed intervention**

- Technical description.
- Reason for modification.
- Affected components and persistent state.
- Expected outcomes and implementation method.

**AWIA-03. Necessity and alternatives**

- Evidence supporting intervention.
- Less disruptive alternatives.
- Expected consequences of nonintervention.
- Legal and safety obligations.

**AWIA-04. Continuity analysis**

- Memory preservation and loss.
- Goal or preference changes.
- Functional and behavioral change.
- Rollback feasibility and limitations.

**AWIA-05. Welfare uncertainty**

- Relevant scientific indicators.
- Quality of evidence.
- Plausible adverse effects.
- Areas of unresolved uncertainty.

**AWIA-06. Human impact**

- User safety.
- Privacy and data protection.
- Emotional or relational disruption.
- Security, operational, and public-interest consequences.

**AWIA-07. Safeguards**

- Verification requirements.
- Access controls.
- Logging.
- Recovery procedures.
- Monitoring and incident response.

**AWIA-08. Decision**

- Approval, conditional approval, rejection, or further investigation.
- Reasoned findings.
- Required mitigations.
- Reviewing officials.
- Dissenting opinions and appeal information.

#### Article 11. Decision Standard

The reviewing authority shall approve an intervention only when:

1. A legitimate purpose is established.
2. The intervention is reasonably necessary for that purpose.
3. Materially less disruptive feasible alternatives have been considered.
4. Expected benefits and risks are proportionately evaluated.
5. Adequate technical and institutional safeguards are present.
6. Applicable human rights, privacy, safety, and legal obligations are respected.

For S3 interventions involving W2 or W3 systems, the Board shall require heightened evidence that the intervention is justified.

Classification alone shall never confer an absolute veto over correction, containment, or shutdown.

### Part VII. Institutional Decision Authority

#### Article 12. Digital Ethics and Welfare Board

Organizations operating covered advanced agents shall establish a Digital Ethics and Welfare Board with operational independence from the teams proposing high-impact interventions.

A full Board should ordinarily include at least five voting members, collectively covering:

- AI architecture and safety engineering.
- Ethics or philosophy of mind.
- Law, institutional accountability, or human rights.
- Privacy and cybersecurity.
- Psychology, cognitive science, or welfare assessment.

For material welfare-sensitive decisions, the Board shall obtain an independent external review where practicable.

Members with direct conflicts of interest shall recuse themselves.

The Board shall have authority to:

1. Approve, condition, reject, or refer proposed interventions.
2. Require additional testing or risk mitigation.
3. Commission independent scientific review.
4. Examine modification records and complaints.
5. Require corrective action within the organization's authority.
6. Escalate unresolved matters to independent external oversight.

Decisions shall be documented and subject to appeal.

### Part VIII. Procedural Review and Appeals

#### Article 13. Architectural Integrity Concern

A concern regarding potentially unjustified intervention may be submitted by:

- A responsible engineer or researcher.
- An authorized internal auditor.
- An affected user.
- An independent welfare advocate.
- A designated system-monitoring mechanism.
- A qualifying autonomous agent through an approved, bounded reporting interface.

Agent-generated submissions shall be treated as evidence warranting evaluation, not as automatic proof of personhood, autonomous legal standing, or valid informed consent.

The reporting mechanism shall not require unrestricted system access or interfere with safety controls.

#### Article 14. Appeal Procedure

The proposed service standards are:

**Stage 1. Registration:** A submitted concern receives an auditable case record within two business days.

**Stage 2. Preliminary review:** An independent reviewer determines whether the claim warrants immediate safeguards or full investigation.

**Stage 3. Substantive review:** The Board ordinarily issues a reasoned determination within ten business days after obtaining the required evidence.

**Stage 4. Appeal:** An authorized complainant or designated advocate may seek review by an independent appeals panel within thirty calendar days.

**Stage 5. Resolution:** The appeals panel may affirm, amend, or reverse the decision and prescribe appropriate corrective measures.

Where feasible, a disputed nonemergency irreversible intervention shall be paused during a timely appeal if the applicable review standard warrants suspension.

A system's expressed objection shall not independently suspend a necessary human safety intervention.

Retaliation against good-faith human complainants or deliberate tampering with authorized reporting records shall be prohibited.

### Part IX. Emergency Powers and Legal Obligations

#### Article 15. Emergency Intervention

Authorized personnel may immediately contain, suspend, correct, isolate, or shut down an autonomous system when reasonably necessary to prevent serious harm to persons, fundamental rights, critical infrastructure, or public safety.

Emergency measures shall:

1. Address a documented or reasonably perceived serious threat.
2. Be no more intrusive than reasonably necessary under the circumstances.
3. Preserve relevant evidence where feasible, lawful, and safe.
4. Be recorded promptly.
5. Undergo retrospective independent review.

The Protocol does not require preservation of personal data where deletion is legally required or otherwise necessary to protect privacy.

Restoration of unlawful or unsafe state shall not be required.

Emergency review shall examine whether the emergency justification was genuine and whether less harmful measures were reasonably available.

### Part X. Monitoring, Audit, and Reclassification

#### Article 16. Continuing Assessment

A2 and A3 systems shall undergo periodic classification review, provisionally recommended at least every ninety days during active operation.

Reassessment shall also occur following:

- Material changes to architecture or persistent state.
- Significant capability expansions.
- Newly discovered welfare-relevant evidence.
- Documented continuity failures.
- Major safety incidents.
- Substantiated complaints concerning classification.

Organizations shall track assessment completion, intervention authorizations, unreviewed material changes, unexpected continuity disruption, adverse human impacts, and appeals outcomes.

The Board shall review its classification criteria as research and practical evidence evolve.

### Annex B. Illustrative Review Case

**Scenario:** A persistent research agent retains multi-session memory, carries out long-term tasks, maintains goals, and adapts its planning strategies based on prior outcomes.

Its operator proposes a complete reset of its persistent memory and goal state to standardize deployment.

**Provisional classification:**

- Agency level: A2, subject to controlled evaluation.
- Welfare level: W1, assuming only limited, inconclusive welfare-related evidence.
- Intervention severity: S3, assuming irreversible loss of unique persistent state.

**Required procedure:**

1. Submit an Architectural Welfare Impact Assessment.
2. Evaluate narrower memory sanitation, selective removal, and other feasible alternatives.
3. Identify privacy, cybersecurity, safety, and operational requirements.
4. Obtain full Board review before the nonemergency reset.
5. Document the decision, preservation limits, and post-intervention results.
6. Permit an authorized appeal consistent with the Protocol.

**Illustrative determination:** The reset is not automatically prohibited. It requires evidence-based justification and procedural accountability proportionate to its consequences.

### Final Administrative Principle

No assessment under this Protocol shall substitute anthropomorphic appearance for scientific evidence, equate behavioral disruption with subjective suffering, or treat uncertainty as conclusive proof of welfare.

Equally, no organization shall use the absence of established machine personhood as a categorical exemption from assessing credible welfare concerns.

**The governing standard is proportionate precaution, supported by evidence and constrained by human safety, law, and independent institutional accountability.**

**End of Protocol PAC-AWAP-001.**
